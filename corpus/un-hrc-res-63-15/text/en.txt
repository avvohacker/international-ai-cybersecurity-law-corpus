63/15. Implications of artificial intelligence for the promotion and protection
of human rights
 The Human Rights Council,
 Guided by the purposes and principles of the Charter of the United Nations,
 Reaffirming the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the Vienna Declaration
and Programme of Action, the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, the
International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights and other relevant
international human rights instruments,
 Welcoming the adoption by the General Assembly of the Pact for the Future1 and the
Global Digital Compact annexed thereto, including commitments to foster an inclusive, open,
sustainable, fair, safe and secure digital future for all and to uphold international law,
including international human rights law, throughout the life cy cle of digital and emerging
technologies,
 Recalling all relevant General Assembly and Human Rights Council resolutions ,
including Council resolutions 53/29 of 14 July 2023 and  59/11 of 7 July 2025,  and
emphasizing the importance of coherence and complementarity among relevant United
Nations processes,
 Welcoming the convening of the first Global Dialogue on Artificial Intelligence
Governance in Geneva in July 2026, and taking note of the preliminary report of the
Independent International Scientific Panel on Artificial Intelligence pursuant to General
Assembly resolution 79/325 of 26 August 2025,
 Recalling the Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence adopted by the
General Conference of the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization
on 23 November 2021, and taking note with appreciation of the work of the Office of the
United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, the Human Rights Council Advisory
Committee, the special procedures and other mechanisms of the Council concerning the
human rights implications of artificial intelligence,

 Reaffirming the Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, which set out the
State duty to protect human rights and the responsibility of business enterprises, including
technology companies to respect human rights, in particular by addressing adverse human
rights impacts and tak ing measures to mitigate and prevent abuses related to business
activities, and the need to ensure access to effective remedy for those affected by business -
related human rights abuses,
 Recognizing that artificial intelligence, when adequate human rights safeguards are in
place, has significant potential to contribute to the promotion and protection of human rights
and fundamental freedoms, including the right to development, inter alia , by facilitating
access to information, education, healthcare and public services, enhancing accessibility and
inclusion, advancing scientific research and innovation and supporting efforts to address
societal and global challenges,
 Recognizing with concern that artificial intelligence systems may undermine the full
enjoyment of human rights and fundamental freedoms, including through reproducing and
exacerbating patterns of discrimination, structural inequalities, marginalization, stereotypes,
biases and exc lusion, infringements of the right to privacy, arbitrary and unlawful
surveillance, undermining information integrity and access to information, intensifying
threats arising from information manipulation , as well as large -scale persuasion, distorting
public debate, eroding trust, affecting free formation of opinion and participation in public
affairs and electoral processes, amplifying different forms of violences and harm and through
the loss of human control over highly autonomous systems,
 Stressing that the use of artificial intelligence systems can, without proper technical,
regulatory, legal and ethical safeguards, affect the enjoyment of human rights, including
economic, social and cultural rights, and pose the risk of reinforcing discrimination, including
structural inequalities, and  recognizing that discriminatory outcomes must be prevented
throughout the life cycle of artificial intelligence, particularly in the administration of justice,
migration governance, the health sector, education, social protection, workplaces and labour
markets through the displacement and disruption of jobs, including in developing countries,
and emphasizing the importance of ensuring that artificial intelligence-enabled economic
transformation contributes to decent work and respects applicable human rights and labour
standards,
 Recognizing that the development, deployment and use of artificial intelligence
systems can have adverse impacts on the enjoyment of a clean, healthy and sustainable
environment throughout their life  cycle, including in relation to energy and water
consumption, greenhouse  gas emissions, the extraction of critical minerals, land use and
electronic waste, and noting with concern that such impacts may be experienced
disproportionately by communities and countries already facing resource scarcity,
environmental degradation and heightened vulnerability to climate change,
 Acknowledging that the adoption of artificial intelligence technology has accelerated
broadly, and unevenly, within and among countries and across sectors, that access to
technologies, as well as risks , are unevenly distributed within and among populations and
countries, while artificial intelligence development and the wealth it creates are highly
concentrated, which could undermine accountability for possible human rights violations and
abuses facilitated by such systems,
 Expressing concern  that developing countries, including least developed countries
and small island developing States, face particular challenges arising from limited access to
digital infrastructure and that digital and artificial intelligence divides and development gaps
within and among countries may exacerbate economic, technological, social, developmental
and environmental inequalities, and recognizing the need to ensure that disparities, within
and among countries, in access to connectivity, computing resources, data, skills, financing
and artificial intelligence capabilities do not prevent individuals, communities and countries
from participating meaningfully in and benefiting from the digital transformation,
 Emphasizing the importance of developing capacities and promoting affordable
access to artificial intelligence technologies, including open-source models, and in this regard
highlighting the potential of artificial intelligence in delivering services at scale and

increasing social and economic opportunities for all through resilient, safe, inclusive and
interoperable digital public infrastructure,
 Recognizing that the implications of artificial intelligence may be experienced
differently by women and girls, children, youth, older persons, persons with disabilities,
persons belonging to national or ethnic, religious and linguistic minorities, migrants, refugees
and asylum-seekers, persons living in poverty and in vulnerable or marginalized situations ,
as well as people living under foreign occupation,
 Expressing concern that the potential risks posed by artificial intelligence systems
may entail different forms of violence and harm against children, including sexual
exploitation, synthetic child abuse material, harmful content, cyberbullying and harassment,
and emphasizing the need to respect, promote and protect the rights of the child and to ensure
that the best interests of the child are a primary consideration throughout the life  cycle of
artificial intelligence,
 Expressing concern also that artificial intelligence systems, including those relying
on automated profiling, biometric technologies, predictive analytics and algorithmic
decision-making, may reproduce, reinforce or amplify existing patterns of racism, racial
discrimination, xeno phobia and other related intolerance, including intolerance and
discrimination based on religion or belief, as well as advocacy of national, racial or religious
hatred that constitutes incitement to discrimination, hostility or violence, particularly where
they are developed or deployed on the basis of biased, incomplete or unrepresentative data
or without adequate human rights safeguards, and emphasizing the importance of preventing
discriminatory outcomes, ensuring appropriate transparency and accountabil ity, and
promoting representative data sets and inclusive design throughout the life cycle of artificial
intelligence systems,
 Recognizing that linguistic and cultural diversity constitute an essential dimension of
inclusive and human -centred artificial intelligence, and noting with concern  that the
underrepresentation of low-resource languages, local knowledge systems and diverse cultural
contexts in data  sets and artificial intelligence models may reinforce existing inequalities,
exclusion and bias,
 Recognizing also that the collection, processing and use of data, often relating to
personal data, including biometric data and data on an individual’s behaviour, social
relationships, race or ethnicity, religion or belief, in the development and operation of
artificial in telligence systems, including for the development of large -scale generative
artificial intelligence systems, without adequate human rights safeguards, can pose serious
risks to the enjoyment of data protection, access to information, the right to privacy, equality
and non-discrimination and other human rights,
 Emphasizing that human dignity and human agency should remain central to the
design, development, deployment, use and governance of artificial intelligence and stressing
the importance of protecting the right to privacy and promoting equality and non -
discrimination, transparency and explainability, accountability, accessibility, appropriate
safeguards, meaningful human oversight and access to effective remedies in addressi ng its
human rights implications, in accordance with States’ obligations under international human
rights law,
 Recognizing the importance of human rights due diligence and appropriate impact
assessments to assist States and business enterprises, including technology companies, in
accordance with the ir respective roles and responsibilities, in identifying, preventing,
mitigating and addressing actual and potential adverse human rights impacts associated with
artificial intelligence systems,
 Emphasizing the important contribution of meaningful participation and dialogue
involving States, regional organizations, national human rights institutions, civil society,
academia, the technical community, business enterprises, affected individuals and
communities and other relevant stakeholders in considering the human rights implications of
artificial intelligence in accordance with their respective roles and responsibilities,
 Emphasizing also the importance of international cooperation, capacity -building,
technical assistance, knowledge-sharing, the transfer of technology on mutually agreed terms

 1. Recognizes the potential of artificial intelligence to contribute significantly to
the promotion and protection of human rights and sustainable development, while
acknowledging that its development and use can also present risks and challenges to the
enjoyment of human rights;
 2. Affirms that the design, development, deployment and use of artificial
intelligence must be consistent with international law, including the purposes and principles
of the Charter of the United Nations, international human rights law and international
humanitarian law, as applicable, and reaffirms in this regard, that States must protect against
human rights abuses, while business enterprises , including technology companies, have the
responsibility to respect human rights;
 3. Underscores that artificial intelligence governance should be in accordance
with international human rights law, as well as international humanitarian law as applicable,
and guided by the principles enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations , including
international cooperation, legality, sovereign equality and non-discrimination;
 4. Calls upon States to consider the implications of artificial intelligence for the
enjoyment of all human rights when developing and implementing relevant legislative,
regulatory, administrative and policy frameworks , and to take appropriate measures to
prevent, mitigate and address actual or potential discrimination and other adverse human
rights impacts that may arise throughout the life cycle of artificial intelligence systems;
 5. Also c alls upon  States to take into account different national contexts,
capacities and levels of development when discussing artificial intelligence governance and
to expand the participation of all countries, in particular developing countries, in order to
harness the benefits and effectively participate in the design, development, deployment and
use of safe, secure, trustworthy and human -centred artificial intelligence systems that
contribute to the full enjoyment of human rights and sustainable development;
 6. Encourages States to ensure access to information throughout the life cycle of
artificial intelligence, particularly concerning artificial intelligence in public functions, and
recognizes that effective partnerships and cooperation between Governments and other
stakeholders is necessary in developing governance approaches and frameworks;
 7. Also e ncourages States to adopt and promote approaches to artificial
intelligence that respect human dignity and human agency and to uphold the principles of
equality and non -discrimination, privacy, accessibility, transparency and explainability,
accountability, sustainability, safety, human oversight and access to effective remedies in
accordance with their obligations under international human rights law and , where
applicable, under international humanitarian law;
 8. Recognizes the necessity to prevent harm to individuals caused by artificial
intelligence systems and to refrain from or cease the use of artificial intelligence applications
that are impossible to operate in compliance with international human rights law or that pose
undue risks to the enjoyment of human rights, especially of those in vulnerable situations,
including by systems whose capabilities could reasonably be expected to cause the loss of
human control;
 9. Encourages States, as appropriate and commensurate with the nature, context
and level of risk, to carry out human rights due diligence and appropriate impact assessments
in relation to artificial intelligence systems that may affect the enjoyment of human rights;
 10. Calls upon  States, where artificial intelligence plays a material role in
decisions having implications for the enjoyment of human rights, and as appropriate to the
nature and level of risk, to put in place mechanisms that ensure meaningful human oversight
and accou ntability and allow affected persons to receive information concerning such
decisions, obtain human review and have access to effective remedies;

 11. Also calls upon States to respect, protect and promote the rights to freedom of
opinion and expression , including the  right of freedom to seek, receive and impart
information, and the right to privacy in the context of artificial intelligence and to ensure that
measures aimed at addressing harmful effects of artificial intelligence are consistent with
international human rights law, including articles 19 and 20 of the International Covenant on
Civil and Political Rights;
 12. Encourages States to pay particular attention to possible differentiated or
disproportionate impacts of artificial intelligence systems on women and girls, children,
youth, older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to national or ethnic,
religious and linguistic minorities, migrants, refugees, asylum -seekers, persons living in
poverty or in vulnerable or marginalized situations and people living under foreign
occupation and to facilitate their meaningful participation in relevant policymaking
processes;
 13. Reminds business enterprises involved in the pre-design, design, development,
provision, procurement, deployment, use and decommissioning of artificial intelligence
systems to respect human rights in accordance with the Guiding Principles on Business and
Human Rights, including through appropriate human rights due diligence , and to identify,
prevent and mitigate actual and potential adverse human rights impacts associated with their
artificial intelligence-related activities and to provide for or cooperate in remediation where
they have caused or contributed to such impacts;
 14. Recognizes the importance of promoting digital and artificial intelligence
literacy and awareness of the opportunities, limitations and potential human rights
implications associated with artificial intelligence;
 15. Calls for strengthened international cooperation and capacity -building,
particularly for developing countries and in accordance with their national needs and
priorities, including through policy exchanges, knowledge-sharing activities and the transfer
of technology on mutually agreed terms, technical assistance, lifelong learning, personnel
training, skilling of workforce, international research cooperation , including cooperation to
develop resources, data  sets and tools for underrepresented languages and knowledge
systems, voluntary joint international research laboratories and artificial intelligence
capacity-building centres, to enable them to harness the benefits of artificial intelligence
while preventing and addressing its potential adverse implications for human rights, and to
support developing countries’ effective, equitable and meaningful participation and
representation in international processes and forums on the governance of artificial
intelligence systems and its implications for human rights;
 16. Encourages States, relevant United Nations entities and other stakeholders, as
appropriate, to promote digital public goods, digital public infrastructure and open artificial
intelligence models, data sets and tools, accompanied by adequate human rights safeguards,
as a means of enabling countries with limited resources, to benefit from these technologies;
 17. Encourages greater coherence and complementarity among relevant
international, regional, national and local initiatives on artificial intelligence, while
recognizing the important role of the United Nations in promoting inclusive international
dialogue and cooperation in this field;
 18. Requests the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human
Rights to continue to contribute, within its mandate, human rights expertise to relevant United
Nations discussions and processes concerning artificial intelligence, while promoting
coherence, coordination and complementarity and avoiding unnecessary duplication with a
view to promoting artificial intelligence governance consistent with international human
rights law;
 19. Encourages the Office of the High Commissioner and relevant United Nations
entities, within their respective mandates and upon the request of States, to support capacity-
building and technical assistance, particularly for developing countries, aimed at
strengthening their capacity to assess and address the human rights implications of artificial
intelligence systems, including through the sharing of kn owledge, experiences and good

 20. Decides to convene, at its sixty-fifth session, a panel discussion on the theme
