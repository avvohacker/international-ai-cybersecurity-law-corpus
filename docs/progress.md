**What the numbers mean.**

- The **60 AI-domain** records are not 60 AI treaties. They are 2 AI-specific treaties, 5 trade agreements with AI provisions, 2 general background treaties, 8 technical standards, 2 implementation texts, 1 negotiation record, 1 EU internal-law act and 39 other non-binding texts, including the three pan-Arab instruments, the two Ibero-American instruments and the three REAIM outcomes. “AI-domain” is a domain tag, not a legal category; the catalogue's `category` field is the legal partition.
- The **15 cyber-only** records are 9 cyber treaties, 3 internal-law acts (NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the ECOWAS Directive) and 3 non-binding instruments (the Paris Call, OECD/LEGAL/0456 and the Pall Mall Code). No record carries both domains.
- The **18 international instruments with binding form** split 9 AI and 9 cyber. The four internal-law acts are binding in their own legal orders, not in international law. Of the 18, 13 are in force and 5 are not confirmed in force: CETS 225, WAICO, the AfCFTA Protocol, CETS 224 and the UN Cybercrime Convention.
- The **36 full texts** comprise 10 `full_text_checked` records and 26 `metadata_verified` texts; the other 39 records are metadata-only. The 36 records with an acquired original have 42 stored files; each file has a URL, access date, byte count and SHA-256 in `catalog/acquisitions.json`.

- The Council of Europe Recommendation CM/Rec(2020)1 is catalogued as a non-binding recommendation.

The GCC Guiding Manual on AI Ethics and the Arab Model Law on AI were not added because no official status or text was verified.
- The Counter Ransomware Initiative, OSCE, Global Cyber Leadership Initiative and Accra Call were checked and not added: they publish a membership, an agency network or a mixed endorser group rather than a stable list of State supporters.
- The Paris Call for AI Safety and Security and the Call for Control of Frontier AI Models are not catalogued as intergovernmental instruments; the first is multi-stakeholder and open-ended.
- Collective statements are in the corpus as collective texts. They are not mapped when the issuer is a fixed membership group whose members are already listed: a map would only repeat the group's composition without adding an individual act.
- Standards are not treaties and are not treated as binding international law. The eight technical-standard records (five ISO/IEC standards, ITU-T Y.3172, IEEE 7000-2021 and CEN EN 18286:2026) are metadata-only.

### Text acquisition audit (25 November 2026)

`python3 tools/audit_texts.py` classifies every record by whether its full text is stored. The result is: **36 stored**, **26 with an official text located but not stored**, **3 internal-law acts** (official texts on EUR-Lex), **5 paywalled ISO/IEC standards** and **2 summary-only records** (the NATO AI strategy summary and the UK–US MOU summary). The 26 are the remaining metadata-only layer: **24 non-binding instruments** (the three pan-Arab, two Ibero-American and three REAIM records, seven other political declarations — including the G7 Digital and Technology Ministerial Declaration of 29 May 2026 — and nine further soft-law, strategy or code texts), **one draft negotiating text** (the CCW GGE on lethal autonomous weapons systems) and **one preparatory/interpretive text** (the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice). The digital trade agreements and the two general background treaties are not in this set: their texts are stored. Their ids and first official source are in `build/text-availability-audit.json`. The classification itself is offline and is covered by `make test`.

`python3 tools/audit_texts.py --probe` performs a real, capped GET on the first official source of each located record and records the result under `download` in the same JSON; the 25 November 2026 run covered the 25 records then classified as located, including the eight regional and REAIM additions; the G7 declaration added later is also located but was not probed. The probe stamps the date of the run, not a fixed date, and a later run without `--probe` merges the `download` results already in the output file, but only for records still `official_text_located` whose first official source is the URL that was probed; an acquired record or a changed link does not inherit an old result. Result of that run: **21 downloaded** (a PDF or an HTML page with content), **2 blocked** by an anti-bot response (`rm.coe.int` for the Council of Europe recommendation on algorithmic systems, HTTP 403, and `commerce.gov` for the G20 objectives, HTTP 403), **2 transient** (the Spanish `mintel` PDF timed out and the UN Digital Library for HRC resolution 63/15 answered HTTP 202 with no delivered file) and **0 broken**. The military-AI declaration's former `state.gov` path returned 404; the record now cites the State Department's official 2021–2025 archive and the official PDF text. A blocked or transient result describes the automated download; it is not evidence that the text does not exist. The `state.gov` host is known to answer 404 to automated clients, so the 404 on the old path is not by itself proof that the page was withdrawn: the archive is the first source the probe checked, and the PDF is an additional source.

### Verification infrastructure

- `catalog/instruments/*.json` is the source of truth; `units.jsonl` is generated from `text/en.txt`; CETS 225 also has `units-fr.jsonl` generated from `text/fr.txt`; manifests record every stored file with a role, size and hash.
- Page maps use inclusive `char_end`; unit anchors use half-open `char_end`. The validator checks that a unit starts inside its declared first page and that its last character (`end - 1`) is inside its declared final page. ## 4. How to continue without starting over

1. Read `catalog/instruments/coe-cets-225.json` and `corpus/coe-cets-225/manifest.json` as the reference pattern.
2. Add a record by copying an existing JSON, changing the id/title/sources/verification, and placing any acquired file under `corpus/<id>/source/`.
3. Register the URL and hash in `catalog/acquisitions.json`; run `tools/make_manifest.py`.
4. Extract and segment only if a full text is acquired; otherwise set `text.full_text: false` and keep the original. For A/RES/79/1 use `--mode pact`; for an official HTML page use `--mode html` and, where necessary, `--html-start`/`--html-end`.
5. Run `make test`. Fix errors rather than suppressing them. Update `docs/coverage.md` and this file.
