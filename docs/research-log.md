## Discovery versus verification

Search snippets were used to locate candidates and, in some cases, to read an official page's key statements. They were not treated as a substitute for reading a document. Records distinguish:

- full text stored and checked;
- official page/excerpt read;
- official source merely located.

Secondary literature is used in the atlas's analytical frame and in the curated section 04 reading list (“Off the record”), where each item is attributed to its author. It is not used to establish the legal status, parties or dates of any instrument; those remain based on primary sources.

The candidate register contains 37 entries: 18 included, 9 pending and 10 excluded. The included set now includes the Arab, CIS and SCO cyber treaties, the CoE Recommendation CM/Rec(2020)1, the G20 AI Prosperity Objectives and the ECOWAS Cybercrime Directive, as well as the earlier records. Pending items include the International AI Safety Report, ITU-T Y.3172, IEEE 7000, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, the G7 2026 Digital and Technology Ministerial Declaration and UNGA 81 drafts L.34/L.37. Excluded items include the G20 AI Prosperity Compact (a private-sector pledge), the Paris Call for AI Safety and Security and the broad G20 Innovation Ministerial Statement. The register records the reason for each decision.

## Negative and provisional results

- No adopted treaty specifically dedicated to AI other than CETS 225 and the WAICO Establishment Agreement was located.
- The Human Rights Council's A/HRC/RES/63/15 is a dedicated AI and human-rights resolution, but it is non-binding and has no party list; it does not change the treaty count.
- No public WAICO depositary register or notification was located; China's national approval is not treated as a deposited ratification, and the 38th founding member remains unnamed in the official record.
- No adopted UN framework convention specific to AI was located, and no AI-specific UN Security Council resolution was located.
- The UN GGE and OEWG cyber reports are not mapped: the GGE list is 25 appointed experts and the OEWG was open to all UN member States, so neither is a list of supporters or adherents. This is a scope decision, not a claim that the reports do not exist.
- The G20 AI Principles are the OECD AI Principles as endorsed by the G20, not a separate instrument; the OECD record covers the substance. The 2026 G20 Innovation Ministerial Statement is broad rather than AI-specific; the separate G20 AI Prosperity Compact and Objectives are pending.
