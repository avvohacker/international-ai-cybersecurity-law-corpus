## Explicit gaps

1. **Two AI-specific treaties, neither confirmed in force.** CETS 225 and the WAICO Establishment Agreement are catalogued; the corpus does not establish that no others exist, only that no others were located in this cycle. CETS 225 is not in force: it lacks the five ratifications required by Article 30(3). For WAICO, Article 15 requires three instruments, but no depositary register or notification was located: the number of deposits is unknown, not zero, and entry into force is unconfirmed. The source-family checklist found no third AI-specific treaty.
2. **Only one negotiation record.** The CCW GGE on LAWS is catalogued, but no draft protocol or adopted instrument exists; the CoE drafting history and any UN framework-convention proposal were not acquired. UNGA 81 drafts L.34 and L.37 are pending candidates, not adopted instruments.
3. **Military AI** is covered by non-binding resolutions and a political declaration, not by a binding instrument. CETS 225 excludes national defence; the UN Panel/Dialogue exclude military AI.
4. **CETS 225 party data are captured** at 23 November 2026: 21 signatories and 2 ratifications (Bosnia and Herzegovina, 28 October 2026; EU, 15 May 2026). Declarations by the EU, Norway and Ukraine are reproduced; no reservations or objections are recorded. The treaty is not in force.
5. **Languages.** Texts are stored in English only. French, Spanish and other official versions are recorded but not acquired; no translations were produced.
6. **Standards.** All five ISO/IEC records are metadata-only because the standards are paywalled. No ITU-T or IEEE standard was verified.
7. **National bilateral registries.** No systematic search of national treaty registries (for example the U.S. TIAS, UK treaty series or national gazettes) was completed. A targeted search of official UK, EU and US pages located the UK–US MOU summary and the US–EU TTC commitment, but not a full UK–US MOU text or a standalone US–EU AI-safety agreement.
8. **Latin America, the Middle East and Central Asia** have no verified regional AI instrument in this cycle.
9. **The candidate exclusions** for WTO JSI, CPTPP, USMCA and RCEP are provisional: no full-text AI keyword check was performed.
10. **Search coverage** was English-language and institutional; multilingual searches were limited to the official language pages encountered. This is not a global exhaustive search.
11. **CCW record is metadata-only.** The 2026 report and Review Conference decision were read, but no full text or units are stored; the 2027–2028 negotiating text is not yet available.
12. **The Paris Statement's endorser list is inconsistent.** The official Élysée PDF of 19 February 2025 lists 64 entries; the official page accessed on 23 November 2026 lists 65 (Zimbabwe added). A contemporaneous Canadian release listed 60, including South Africa, the United Arab Emirates and the Holy See (listed as “Vatican”), which are absent from the Élysée lists. No withdrawal or correction is documented.

13. **New Delhi's two official lists disagree.** The MEA page updated 21 February 2026 lists 91 endorsers and omits Colombia; the PIB annex states “as of 5th March 2026” and lists 92, including Colombia. The record uses the later PIB list and itemises 90 States, the EU and IFAD. No official explanation for the difference was located.

14. **BRICS has no individual signatures.** The 2025 statement was issued collectively at the Rio de Janeiro summit and has no signature block. The presidency lists 11 member States; the record counts those listed members, not individually evidenced issuers, and records that the Saudi Press Agency described Saudi Arabia as an invited country. It remains a political statement, not a treaty, and is mapped with a distinct collective-issue status rather than as eleven signatures. The distinction between corpus scope and map scope is set out in the [scope review](methodology.md#10-scope-review-why-two-instruments-were-nearly-missed).

15. **The Human Rights Council layer is now represented, but only by one resolution.** A/HRC/RES/63/15 (6 October 2026) is catalogued as metadata-verified and non-binding. The Council's earlier AI resolutions (53/29 and 59/11) are recalled in its preamble but not separately catalogued.

16. **Current processes are pending, not adopted.** The G7 Digital and Technology Ministerial Declaration (29 May 2026), the G20 AI Prosperity Compact and Objectives (2 September 2026) and UNGA 81 drafts L.34/L.37 are in the candidate register as pending. The Paris Call for AI Safety and Security (9 November 2026) and the broad G20 Innovation Ministerial Statement are excluded with reasons in the candidate register.
17. **China's initiative has no adherence list.** It is a unilateral non-binding proposal, not an agreed instrument; the record does not attribute it to any State other than China.

18. **The transatlantic AI-safety records are not treaties.** The UK–US MOU is non-binding and its full text was not published; the US–EU commitment is one AI section of a broad TTC joint statement and creates no binding obligation. Neither record supports a claim of a binding transatlantic AI-safety regime.
19. **General international law is not comprehensively covered.** The `general_treaty` layer is a deliberately minimal background selection: the VCLT for treaty law and the ICCPR for human rights. The UN Charter, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the ECHR, Convention 108+ and other general instruments are not catalogued; customary international law, general principles, Security Council resolutions (none AI-specific was located) and international humanitarian law are not systematically covered. Their absence is a scope limit, not a finding that they are irrelevant to AI.
20. **WAICO party data are incomplete.** China's MFA reports 38 founding members, but only 37 are individually identified across the official statements; the 38th is recorded as unknown. Bangladesh is not that member: on 1 August 2026 it announced that it would join 'initially as an observer', and Article 11 requires an observer application and Council approval; no admission is recorded. Singapore has been reported as considering an invitation and is not a member. The Government of China is the depositary under Article 18, but no public depositary register, status table or notification was located. China's approval on 6 November 2026 is a domestic authorisation, not a deposited ratification; deposits are recorded as unknown, not zero, and entry into force is unconfirmed. The map therefore shows signatures, not ratifications.

## What “coverage” means here

It means: 47 records (46 AI-layer and the Budapest Convention as the first cybersecurity-only record), each with at least one official source and a declared verification level; 16 stored full texts with hashed originals or transcribed text; 15 acquired originals; one category-6 negotiation record; and a candidate register of 31 entries (11 included, 11 pending, 9 excluded) that records both inclusions and exclusions. The cybersecurity families in the integration plan are not yet catalogued beyond Budapest. It does not mean that every international AI instrument has been found.
