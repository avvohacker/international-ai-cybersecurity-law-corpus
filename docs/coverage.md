## Explicit gaps

1. **Only one AI-specific treaty.** The corpus does not establish that no others exist; it records that none was located in this cycle.
2. **Category 6 is empty.** No draft text or ongoing negotiation was catalogued. The CCW GGE on LAWS is pending; the CoE drafting history and any UN framework-convention proposal were not acquired.
3. **Military AI** is covered by non-binding resolutions and a political declaration, not by a binding instrument. CETS 225 excludes national defence; the UN Panel/Dialogue exclude military AI.
4. **CETS 225 party data** are aggregate only (21 signatories, 2 ratifications at 10 November 2026); the second ratifying party is unverified, and declarations/reservations are not captured.
5. **Languages.** Texts are stored in English only. French, Spanish and other official versions are recorded but not acquired; no translations were produced.
6. **Standards.** All five ISO/IEC records are metadata-only because the standards are paywalled. No ITU-T or IEEE standard was verified.
7. **National bilateral registries.** No systematic search of national treaty registries (for example the U.S. TIAS, UK treaty series or national gazettes) was completed.
8. **Latin America, the Middle East and Central Asia** have no verified regional AI instrument in this cycle.
9. **The candidate exclusions** for WTO JSI, CPTPP, USMCA and RCEP are provisional: no full-text AI keyword check was performed.
10. **Search coverage** was English-language and institutional; multilingual searches were limited to the official language pages encountered. This is not a global exhaustive search.

## What “coverage” means here

It means: 35 records, each with at least one official source and a declared verification level; 14 stored full texts with hashed originals or transcribed text; 13 acquired originals; and a candidate register that records both inclusions and exclusions. It does not mean that every international AI instrument has been found.
