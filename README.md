# international-ai-cybersecurity-law-corpus

Source-verified corpus of international artificial intelligence and cybersecurity law: treaties, digital agreements, declarations, resolutions, standards and authoritative materials. Faithful texts, traceable citations, signatures, ratifications and reservations, reproducible builds and documented global coverage, distinguishing binding form, current entry into force and non-binding status.

**Status: first working corpus, verification date 2026-11-26.** The 77 records currently catalogued comprise 62 AI-domain records and 15 cybersecurity-only records: nine cyber treaties (the Budapest Convention, its two protocols ETS 189 and CETS 224, the African Union Malabo Convention, the UN Cybercrime Convention, the Arab Convention on IT Offences, the CIS 2018 Dushanbe Agreement, the CIS 2001 Minsk Agreement and the SCO 2009 Information Security Agreement), three cyber internal-law acts (NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the ECOWAS Cybercrime Directive), and three non-binding instruments — the Paris Call for Trust and Security in Cyberspace, the OECD Recommendation on Digital Security of Critical Activities (OECD/LEGAL/0456) and the Pall Mall Code of Practice for States. The schema records `domains` and the integration plan sets the scope. This is a documented partial coverage, not a claim of completeness. See [coverage](docs/coverage.md), [remaining work](docs/progress.md) and the [cybersecurity integration plan](docs/cybersecurity-integration-plan.md).

### The two AI-specific treaties

The Council of Europe **Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (CETS No. 225)**, opened for signature at Vilnius on 5 September 2024, is one of two treaties in this corpus whose subject matter is AI as such. It is a framework convention: it sets principles, risk-management duties, remedies and a Conference of the Parties, while leaving implementation to Parties. As of the official chart status of 25 November 2026 it had 21 signatories and 2 ratifications and was **not in force**: Bosnia and Herzegovina ratified on 28 October 2026 and the EU on 15 May 2026 (Council Decision (EU) 2026/1080). Article 30(3) requires five ratifications including at least three Council of Europe member States.

The second is the **Agreement on the Establishment of the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO)**, signed at the inaugural ceremony in Shanghai on 16 July 2026. China's MFA reports 38 founding members; 37 are individually identified in the primary record (29 at the ceremony and eight on 30–31 July 2026), and the 38th is recorded as unknown. The Agreement is limited to civilian AI cooperation and establishes an organisation headquartered in Shanghai. Article 15 requires three instruments of ratification, acceptance or approval. China's National People's Congress approved ratification on 6 November 2026, Nicaragua's Presidency ratified it by decree No. 11-2026 of 16 October 2026 and Russia's Government accepted it by decree No. 1532 of 17 November 2026, but all three are **domestic acts**, recorded separately and not counted as international deposits. Nicaragua's decree orders the instrument of ratification to be issued for deposit with China, but no deposit was verified. No depositary notification or register was located: the number of deposits is **unknown, not zero**, and entry into force is **not confirmed**. A WAICO promotion meeting on 17 July 2026, convened by the NDRC and MFA, was not a Council plenary and did not constitute organs. The record therefore has status `unknown` and is mapped as signature only.

No other adopted treaty specifically dedicated to AI was located in this first search cycle. That is a finding about the coverage achieved, not a claim that none exists.

### Binding agreements with express AI provisions

Five agreements are catalogued in this category:

- **DEPA** (New Zealand, Singapore, Chile, Korea and Costa Rica; in force) — Article 8.2, best-efforts AI governance cooperation. Costa Rica presented its instrument of accession on 17 July 2026; Chile’s SUBREI records the accession as effective on 14 September 2026, while the joint release is dated 17 September 2026. The New Zealand MFAT overview still says four Parties and is stale.
- **SADEA** (Australia–Singapore; in force) — Article 31, AI cooperation and governance frameworks.
- **UKSDEA** (United Kingdom–Singapore; in force) — Article 8.61-R, AI and emerging technologies.
- **KSDPA** (Korea–Singapore; in force) — Article 14.28, AI governance and ethics cooperation.
- **AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol** (African Union; adopted 2024, status unknown) — Article 2(2)(f) objective and an Annex on Emerging and Advanced Technologies whose Articles 4, 5 and 12 use “shall” obligations on deployment, national treatment and technical standards for State Parties once the Protocol is in force for them.

All five are trade/digital agreements. Most AI clauses are programmatic or cooperative; the AfCFTA annex is the exception in wording, but its obligations bind only State Parties and only once the Protocol is in force for them. The AU publishes no ratification table or status list for the Protocol, and the Assembly's February 2026 decision still urged ratification by the end of 2026; ratifications and deposits are unknown, not zero, and no count is asserted.

### Non-binding and other layers

- **UN General Assembly:** nine resolutions, including 78/265 (2024), 79/62 (2024, LAWS), 79/239 (2024, military AI), 79/325 (2025, Scientific Panel and Global Dialogue), and the 2025 LAWS/military-AI resolutions; the Pact for the Future/Global Digital Compact (79/1) is catalogued as an original. The 81st-session drafts L.34 (military AI) and L.37 (LAWS) are pending candidates, not adopted resolutions.
- **Human Rights Council:** A/HRC/RES/63/15 (6 October 2026), a dedicated AI and human-rights resolution adopted without a vote; non-binding and metadata-verified.
- **Summits and State initiatives:** the Paris AI Action Summit Statement (2025), endorsed by 63 States, the EU and the African Union Commission, but not by the United States or the United Kingdom; the New Delhi Declaration (2026), with 92 itemised endorsers; China's Global AI Governance Initiative (2023), a unilateral non-binding proposal with no formal adherence list; the BRICS AI statement (2025), issued collectively by the leaders of 11 listed member States and not individually signed; and the Pax Silica Declaration (12 December 2025), a US-led political declaration with 26 signatories (25 States and the EU) as of 23 October 2026. Pax Silica is non-binding; the separate AI Opportunity Statement (June 2026) is a different instrument and is not counted with it.
- **Political signature:** the San Francisco Declaration on the Secure Foundations of Enduring Prosperity (15 November 2026), signed by ten governments. It is a political text: signature does not make it a treaty.
- **Transatlantic AI safety:** the UK–US MOU (1 April 2024), whose named institutes are now the UK AI Security Institute and NIST's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), and the US–EU TTC joint statement (5 April 2024), which committed the European AI Office and the US AI Safety Institute to a technical Dialogue. The first is an MOU whose full text was not published and for which no renewal or termination was located; the second is a political commitment inside a broader joint statement, not a standalone agreement. No TTC ministerial after September 2024 was located, although the Commission's current TTC page still describes its ten working groups; no US–EU “Digital Dialogue” launch was located as of 20 November 2026: both the Commission's Digital Dialogues page (9 October 2026) and its broader International digital cooperation page (20 November 2026) record no United States dialogue.
- **OECD:** Recommendation on AI (OECD/LEGAL/0449), the first intergovernmental AI standard.
- **UNESCO:** Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021).
- **G7:** Hiroshima Process International Code of Conduct (2023) and the G7 Digital and Technology Ministerial Declaration (Paris, 29 May 2026), whose AI sections commend the revised Hiroshima Process Reporting Framework and set out work on AI risk-assessment comparability, AI openness and AI adoption.
- **Military:** U.S.-led Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy (2023); NATO's revised AI strategy summary (2024); the three REAIM outcome documents — the 2023 Call to Action, the 2024 Blueprint for Action and the 2026 Pathways to Action.
- **Regional:** AU Continental AI Strategy (2024); ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics (2024); the Unified Arab AI Strategy (2025), the Arab Charter on AI Ethics (2025) and the ALECSO Charter on AI Ethics (2025); the CLAD Ibero-American AI Charter (2023) and the 2024 Ibero-American Declaration on Ethical and Trustworthy AI.
- **Standards:** ISO/IEC 42001, 23894, 22989, 23053 and 42006 — metadata only, because the standards are paywalled.
- **Negotiations:** CCW GGE on LAWS — 2024–2026 final report and 2027–2028 negotiating mandate, catalogued as `draft_negotiation`; no adopted instrument.
- **Implementation/interpretation:** CETS 225 Explanatory Report; EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice (2025).
- **Supranational law:** EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689; NIS2 Directive, Directive (EU) 2022/2555; Cyber Resilience Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847. They are collected separately as internal EU law, not as international binding instruments; NIS2 and the CRA are cyber-only, the AI Act is AI-only.
- **General treaties:** ICCPR and the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, selected because they supply the human-rights and treaty-law framework applicable to AI instruments.

## Repository layout

```text
catalog/
  instruments/*.json     one record per instrument (the source of truth)
  candidates.json        candidate register with include/exclude/pending decisions
  acquisitions.json      URLs, access dates and SHA-256 of stored originals
corpus/
  <instrument-id>/
    source/              acquired originals (PDF or HTML), only where rights permit
    text/en.txt          extracted or transcribed text
    pages.json           page-to-character map
    units.jsonl          citable units with exact character and page anchors
    manifest.json        file list, roles, sizes and SHA-256
schema/                  JSON Schemas for instrument and candidate records
tools/                   acquisition, extraction, segmentation, manifest, build and atlas tools
tests/                   integrity, schema, anchor and determinism tests
docs/                    methodology, research log, coverage matrix, progress
data/                    Natural Earth boundary data and organisation membership lists
site/                    GitHub Pages atlas source (CSS, JS); the HTML is built, not tracked
.github/workflows/       GitHub Pages deployment workflow
build/                   generated catalogue, checksums and verification report
```

## Reproduce

The build is offline and deterministic; it needs Python 3.12 and the packages in `requirements-tools.txt` (`jsonschema` for applying the published schema; `pypdf` and `cryptography` only for optional text extraction).

```bash
make validate     # schema and cross-record checks
make build        # build/catalog.json, CATALOG.md, checksums.sha256
make test         # build + 73 integrity/schema/anchor/determinism tests
make site         # build + generate the static atlas in site/
```

Text extraction from PDFs or official HTML needs the optional tooling:

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements-tools.txt
.venv/bin/python tools/extract_text.py <instrument-id> source/<file>.pdf
.venv/bin/python tools/extract_text.py <instrument-id> source/<file>.html --mode html \
  --html-start '<div class="content_text">' --html-end '<!-- Footer  -->'
.venv/bin/python tools/segment.py <instrument-id> --mode treaty|resolution|numbered|whole|pact|pages
.venv/bin/python tools/make_manifest.py
```

Network acquisition is deliberately separate:

```bash
python3 tools/acquire.py --check          # verify stored originals against catalog/acquisitions.json
python3 tools/acquire.py                  # fetch missing originals, never overwriting a registered version
```

`tools/acquire.py` refuses to overwrite a stored source. If the remote bytes differ from the registered hash, it saves the new bytes under the access date and fails, so that a version change is visible rather than silent.

## Human-readable atlas (GitHub Pages)

The generated page is a consultable public catalogue. It opens with a clickable index (00) and section 01, “Overlook”, which gives four indicators (77 records, 18 treaty-based international instruments, 55 non-binding, seventeen maps) and a legal-form bar split into 13 international instruments in force, 5 not confirmed in force, 4 internal-law acts and 55 non-binding records, then a short breakdown of the 62 AI-domain and 15 cyber-only records. The catalogue's category column gives the legal partition; the page does not draw a separate three-set diagram. Section 02 holds the seventeen State-level maps, ordered treaty-based AI, treaty-based cyber, non-binding AI, non-binding cyber. Section 03 is a single chronology of all 77 records and 11 events: every record with a State-level map is a box whose title opens that map, while records without a map and events remain plain rows; each row still states its legal form and whether it is in force. Section 04 is “Reading room” in the index and navigation, with “Off the record” as its heading: a curated layer of legal doctrine, think-tank analysis and civil-society critique, attributed and kept separate from the primary sources. Section 05 is the filterable catalogue, section 06 records method and limits, and section 07 collects the repository, the official source portals and the archives behind the records. Each map carries the instrument’s full official title, the participation count, and a panel with its description, dates, history and official-source links, all read from the record. The page is built from the same records, with no editorial database of its own; the membership lists used by the maps are in `data/organisations.json` and the unseen events in `data/unseen-events.json`.

```bash
make site        # validates, builds and regenerates site/index.html
```

The official links used by the seventeen instrument panels, and all official source links in the records, can be audited separately:

```bash
make audit-links          # seventeen map panels; writes build/link-audit.json
make audit-catalog-links  # every source entry in all records, non-official included
make audit-doc-links      # external citations in docs/international-ai-law.md
```

The panel run writes `build/link-audit.json`; the catalog-wide run writes `build/catalog-link-audit.json`; `make audit-acquisitions` audits the fifteen acquired instruments, including the non-official reproductions listed in section 07, and writes `build/acquisition-link-audit.json` with the audit date in the report; `make audit-doc-links` writes `build/doc-link-audit.json`. All audits are network-only and classify broken links separately from anti-bot blocks and transient errors; `make test` never touches the network. The catalog-wide and acquisition audits run with two workers because `web.archive.org` returns connection errors to eight concurrent probes, which the classifier would report as broken links. The 26 November 2026 catalog-wide run, after the fifteen new texts, the WAICO promotion-meeting source, the Nicaraguan gazette source, the Brazil–China AI MOU, the GPAI declaration and the Commission's International digital cooperation source were included, checked 273 entries in all 77 records: 268 official or institutional sources (official primary, official secondary, registry and official journal) and 5 non-institutional reproductions or reports (256 unique URLs). The new Nicaraguan gazette source resolved; the BRICS presidency PDF returned HTTP 429 in this run and is counted anti-bot. The five Brazil–China sources split into two resolved (`mre-mou-pt`, `concordia-faq`) and three anti-bot challenge pages (`mre-acts-page`, `mdic-news`, `mre-atos-internacionais`). Results: 181 resolved, 82 anti-bot, 0 broken and 10 transient; three pages classified as transient were opened in a browser on 25 November 2026 and are counted as `live_but_unreachable`, not as resolved links. The 68 source entries of the fifteen new texts split into 41 resolved, 23 anti-bot, 4 transient and 0 broken. The ten catalog-wide transients are four `eccis.org` URLs, the Ecuadorian `mintel` PDF, three `industry.gov.au` URLs, the `dfat.gov.au` page and the Korean MOFA 307. The blocked group includes nine `state.gov` URLs (HTTP 407), `commerce.gov` (HTTP 403), CoE and `rm.coe.int` (HTTP 403), `docs.un.org` and `meetings.unoda.org` challenges, `mea.gov.in` and `impact.indiaai.gov.in` (HTTP 403), SEGIB (HTTP 403), and ALECSO, AICTO and NIST challenge pages. These are blocked or transient, not broken. A DNS failure is classified as a transient network error, so an unreachable host is never recorded as a withdrawn page. The fifteen-instrument run checked 73 entries: 49 resolved, 21 anti-bot, 0 broken and 3 transient.

The static output lives in `site/`; run `make site` to generate `site/index.html` locally, or publish it with the workflow in `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. The generated HTML is not tracked, so a fresh clone rebuilds it from the records. Pages is already enabled with **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**; the workflow publishes on push to `main`. After the workflow runs, the page is served at `https://avvohacker.github.io/international-ai-cybersecurity-law-corpus/`. The workflow rebuilds the catalogue and the atlas before uploading, so the published page cannot silently drift from the records.

The maps use Natural Earth 1:110m Admin 0 Countries (public domain) and the membership lists in `data/organisations.json`. No State names are drawn inside the maps. An international organisation that is a party is named below the map title, outside the map frame; its member States keep a pale, light fill only where they did not act individually, so the level stays distinct from a signature and from a ratification in every palette. Section 07 lists every source cited by a record, official and non-official, so the record can be checked from the page itself. The analytical section separates regulatory models, institutional vehicles and diplomatic coalitions; it treats them as readings of the record set, not as legal classifications. The page says so.

## Verification levels

Every record declares one of:

- `full_text_checked` (11): a full text is stored, hashed, segmented and inspected; key passages are asserted in tests.
- `metadata_verified` (65): the official page or official excerpt was read during this session; a full text may be stored but was not read end-to-end.
- `source_located` (0): an official URL is recorded, but the metadata comes from an established reference and was not re-read in this session.

`metadata_verified` does **not** mean every field was independently verified. Party lists, reservations and some status details remain explicitly unknown. See [docs/methodology.md](docs/methodology.md).

## What is deliberately absent

- National legislation. The EU AI Act, NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act are collected separately as EU internal law, not as international instruments.
- Academic literature and news commentary.
- A dynamic interface, API or server. The static GitHub Pages atlas is a generated view of the records, not a database-backed service.
- Automatic translations presented as authentic texts.
- Any claim to have searched the whole internet or to have found every existing instrument.
- General international law beyond the two background treaties (VCLT and ICCPR). The UN Charter, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the ECHR, Convention 108+ and international humanitarian law are not catalogued; customary international law, general principles and Security Council resolutions (none AI-specific was located) are not systematically covered. This is a scope limit, not a finding that they are irrelevant to AI.
- Only one negotiation record (the CCW GGE on LAWS); there is no adopted draft protocol and no UN framework convention.

## Known limitations

1. **CETS 225 is not in force.** Its party table is captured at the Treaty Office status of 25 November 2026 (21 signatories; Bosnia and Herzegovina and the EU have ratified), but no Conference of the Parties exists and implementation is not assessed. The Treaty Office publishes the chart dynamically and exposes no static export; a local JSON snapshot of the official chart/declarations is stored at `corpus/coe-cets-225/status/coe-cets-225-status-2026-11-23.json` and hashed in the manifest.
2. **Two official PDF endpoints remain blocked:** the direct CoE link for CETS 225 and the UNESCO UNESDOC link. The Explanatory Report and the UNESCO Recommendation were acquired from archived copies of the official PDFs; for CETS 225, a 12-page official reproduction and the certified bilingual copy in the corrected French version dated 20 May 2025 are stored. The English text remains a transcription because the direct CoE endpoint returns HTTP 403.
3. **EUR-Lex PDFs returned HTTP 202/empty.** The EU AI Act and the EU OJ version of CETS 225 are catalogued with metadata, not stored full texts.
4. **Most stored texts are English, but not all.** The Unified Arab AI Strategy is stored in Arabic, the CLAD Charter and the Ibero-American Declaration in Spanish, the CIS 2018 Agreement in Russian and WAICO in Chinese. The authentic French text of CETS 225 is also stored (`text/fr.txt`) and segmented into 36 article units (`units-fr.jsonl`); other authentic versions are recorded in metadata but not acquired.
5. **Many records are metadata-level.** 15 of 77 instruments are catalogued without a stored full text; 50 of the 62 stored texts were not read end-to-end. The distinctions are machine-readable.
6. **Only one negotiation record** (the CCW GGE); no adopted draft protocol and no bilateral AI treaty were verified. Full-text checks on 26 November 2026 found no express AI provision in the CPTPP, the EU–Chile ITA and AFA, the EU–Mercosur iTA, the Pacific Alliance Additional Protocol, the Pacific Alliance–Singapore FTA, the Mercosur–Singapore FTA or the Mercosur E-Commerce Agreement; DEPA remains the only Latin American trade agreement in the corpus with an AI clause. A Brazil–China AI MOU (13 May 2025) is catalogued as a non-binding interinstitutional act, not a treaty; its entry-into-force clause is the effective date of the cooperation arrangement. Latin America and the Middle East now have non-binding regional AI instruments, but no binding regional AI treaty outside the EU AI Act; Central Asia has no dedicated regional AI instrument in this cycle.
7. **Printed pagination vs file pagination** is modelled only partially: unit `page_start`/`page_end` are PDF file pages, not printed page numbers; CETS 225's English units use the 12 pages of the acquired official reproduction; its French units use a 13-page sequence that records the corresponding pages (3, 5, ..., 27) of the certified bilingual PDF.
8. **The cybersecurity layer is broader but still partial.** Fifteen cybersecurity-only records are catalogued: nine binding treaties (the Budapest Convention, ETS 189, CETS 224, the AU Malabo Convention, the UN Cybercrime Convention, the Arab Convention on IT Offences, the CIS 2018 Dushanbe Agreement, the CIS 2001 Minsk Agreement and the SCO 2009 Information Security Agreement), three internal-law acts (NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the ECOWAS Cybercrime Directive) and three non-binding instruments (the Paris Call, the OECD Digital Security Recommendation and the Pall Mall Code). The five treaties with stable itemised depositary tables are mapped (Budapest, ETS 189, CETS 224, Malabo and the UN Convention); the Arab, CIS and SCO treaties have stored full texts but are unmapped because their depositary tables are not stable or public. The three non-binding texts are mapped from their official State lists (Paris Call: 81 States and the EU; OECD: 39 adherents; Pall Mall: 27 supporting States). The Budapest and protocol reservation and declaration pages are reproduced in the records (Budapest: 96 reservations and 280 declarations; ETS 189: 21 reservations, 11 declarations and one communication; CETS 224: one reservation and 21 declarations), and the UNTC panel supplies 11 reservation paragraphs and four declaration paragraphs for the UN Convention. Malabo is in force but its official status list is dated 2 February 2026; the UN Convention is not in force. Cyber standards and cooperation channels remain pending. See [docs/cybersecurity-integration-plan.md](docs/cybersecurity-integration-plan.md).

## Summary

The corpus catalogues 77 written instruments: 62 AI-domain records and 15 cybersecurity-only records. Legal form and current force are kept separate: 18 international instruments have binding form, of which 13 are in force and 5 are not confirmed in force; four further acts are internal law, and 55 records are non-binding. Sixty-two full texts and 17 State-level maps are stored or generated.

The corpus records no universal binding AI treaty in force: CETS 225 is not in force and WAICO's entry into force is unconfirmed. The other binding AI provisions sit in digital trade agreements, while declarations, resolutions, standards and political coalitions remain non-binding. The seventeen maps show that treaty participation and political endorsement cut across the three-pole image; they do not create legal blocs. This is a documented partial coverage, not a claim of completeness: gaps, searches and checks are recorded in `docs/`.
