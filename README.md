# international-ai-law-corpus

Source-verified corpus of international artificial intelligence law: treaties, digital agreements, declarations, resolutions, standards and authoritative materials. Faithful texts, traceable citations, signatures, ratifications and reservations, reproducible builds and documented global coverage, distinguishing binding and non-binding instruments.

**Status: first working corpus, verification date 2026-11-23.** This is a documented partial coverage, not a claim of completeness. See [coverage](docs/coverage.md) and [remaining work](docs/progress.md).

Stored full texts (16): CETS 225; UNGA 78/241, 78/265, 78/311, 79/1, 79/62, 79/239, 79/325, 80/57 and 80/58; DEPA; SADEA; the G7 Hiroshima Code; the OECD AI Recommendation; the Paris AI Action Summit Statement; and China's Global AI Governance Initiative. Fifteen are backed by a stored original (14 PDFs and the China HTML); CETS 225 is a transcription of the official text because its PDF returned HTTP 403.

### The two AI-specific treaties

The Council of Europe **Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (CETS No. 225)**, opened for signature at Vilnius on 5 September 2024, is one of two treaties in this corpus whose subject matter is AI as such. It is a framework convention: it sets principles, risk-management duties, remedies and a Conference of the Parties, while leaving implementation to Parties. As of the official chart status of 23 November 2026 it had 21 signatories and 2 ratifications and was **not in force**: Bosnia and Herzegovina ratified on 28 October 2026 and the EU on 15 May 2026 (Council Decision (EU) 2026/1080). Article 30(3) requires five ratifications including at least three Council of Europe member States.

The second is the **Agreement on the Establishment of the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO)**, signed at the inaugural ceremony in Shanghai on 16 July 2026. China's MFA reports 38 founding members; 37 are individually identified in the primary record (29 at the ceremony and eight on 30–31 July 2026), and the 38th is recorded as unknown. The Agreement is limited to civilian AI cooperation and establishes an organisation headquartered in Shanghai. Article 15 requires three instruments of ratification, acceptance or approval. China's National People's Congress approved ratification on 6 November 2026, but no deposit was verified; the Agreement is therefore **not in force** and is mapped as signature only.

No other adopted treaty specifically dedicated to AI was located in this first search cycle. That is a finding about the coverage achieved, not a claim that none exists.

### Binding agreements with express AI provisions

Five agreements are catalogued in this category:

- **DEPA** (New Zealand, Chile, Singapore; in force) — Article 8.2, best-efforts AI governance cooperation.
- **SADEA** (Australia–Singapore; in force) — Article 31, AI cooperation and governance frameworks.
- **UKSDEA** (United Kingdom–Singapore; in force) — Article 8.61-R, AI and emerging technologies.
- **KSDPA** (Korea–Singapore; in force) — Article 14.28, AI governance and ethics cooperation.
- **AfCFTA Digital Trade Protocol** (African Union; adopted 2024, not in force) — emerging and advanced technologies objective and annex.

All five are trade/digital agreements. Their AI clauses are programmatic or cooperative; none imposes a substantive AI product rule.

### Non-binding and other layers

- **UN General Assembly:** nine resolutions, including 78/265 (2024), 79/62 (2024, LAWS), 79/239 (2024, military AI), 79/325 (2025, Scientific Panel and Global Dialogue), and the 2025 LAWS/military-AI resolutions; the Pact for the Future/Global Digital Compact (79/1) is catalogued as an original.
- **Summits and State initiatives:** the Paris AI Action Summit Statement (2025), endorsed by 63 States, the EU and the African Union Commission, but not by the United States or the United Kingdom; China's Global AI Governance Initiative (2023), a unilateral non-binding proposal with no formal adherence list; and the Pax Silica Declaration (12 December 2025), a US-led political declaration with 26 signatories (25 States and the EU) as of 23 October 2026. Pax Silica is non-binding; the separate AI Opportunity Statement (June 2026) is a different instrument and is not counted with it.
- **Transatlantic AI safety:** the UK–US MOU (1 April 2024), implemented by the two AI Safety Institutes, and the US–EU TTC joint statement (5 April 2024), which commits the European AI Office and the US AI Safety Institute to a technical Dialogue. The first is an MOU whose full text was not published; the second is a political commitment inside a broader joint statement, not a standalone agreement.
- **OECD:** Recommendation on AI (OECD/LEGAL/0449), the first intergovernmental AI standard.
- **UNESCO:** Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021).
- **G7:** Hiroshima Process International Code of Conduct (2023).
- **Military:** U.S.-led Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy (2023); NATO's revised AI strategy summary (2024).
- **Regional:** AU Continental AI Strategy (2024); ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics (2024).
- **Standards:** ISO/IEC 42001, 23894, 22989, 23053 and 42006 — metadata only, because the standards are paywalled.
- **Negotiations:** CCW GGE on LAWS — 2024–2026 final report and 2027–2028 negotiating mandate, catalogued as `draft_negotiation`; no adopted instrument.
- **Implementation/interpretation:** CETS 225 Explanatory Report; EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice (2025).
- **Supranational law:** EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, collected separately.
- **General treaties:** ICCPR and the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, selected because they supply the human-rights and treaty-law framework applicable to AI instruments.

## Repository layout

```text
catalog/
  instruments/*.json     one record per instrument (the source of truth)
  candidates.json        candidate register with include/exclude/pending decisions
  acquisitions.json      URLs, access dates and SHA-256 of stored originals
corpus/
  <instrument-id>/
    source/              acquired originals (PDF or HTML), only where rights permit
    text/en.txt          extracted or transcribed text
    pages.json           page-to-character map
    units.jsonl          citable units with exact character and page anchors
    manifest.json        file list, roles, sizes and SHA-256
schema/                  JSON Schemas for instrument and candidate records
tools/                   acquisition, extraction, segmentation, manifest, build and atlas tools
tests/                   integrity, schema, anchor and determinism tests
docs/                    methodology, research log, coverage matrix, progress
data/                    Natural Earth boundary data and organisation membership lists
site/                    GitHub Pages atlas source (CSS, JS); the HTML is built, not tracked
.github/workflows/       GitHub Pages deployment workflow
build/                   generated catalogue, checksums and verification report
```

## Reproduce

The build is offline and deterministic; it needs only Python 3.12 and the standard library.

```bash
make validate     # schema and cross-record checks
make build        # build/catalog.json, CATALOG.md, checksums.sha256
make test         # build + 29 integrity/determinism tests
make site         # build + generate the static atlas in site/
```

Text extraction from PDFs or official HTML needs the optional tooling:

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements-tools.txt
.venv/bin/python tools/extract_text.py <instrument-id> source/<file>.pdf
.venv/bin/python tools/extract_text.py <instrument-id> source/<file>.html --mode html \
  --html-start '<div class="content_text">' --html-end '<!-- Footer  -->'
.venv/bin/python tools/segment.py <instrument-id> --mode treaty|resolution|numbered|whole|pact
.venv/bin/python tools/make_manifest.py
```

Network acquisition is deliberately separate:

```bash
python3 tools/acquire.py --check          # verify stored originals against catalog/acquisitions.json
python3 tools/acquire.py                  # fetch missing originals, never overwriting a registered version
```

`tools/acquire.py` refuses to overwrite a stored source. If the remote bytes differ from the registered hash, it saves the new bytes under the access date and fails, so that a version change is visible rather than silent.

## Human-readable atlas (GitHub Pages)

The generated page is a consultable public catalogue: counts, seven maps with the recorded parties listed under each map, a chronology, a three-pole heuristic checked against the legal record, and a filterable catalogue. Each map also carries a “What it is” panel with the instrument’s description, dates, history and links to its official sources, all read from the record. It is built from the same records, with no editorial database of its own. The membership lists used by the maps are in `data/organisations.json`, with the official EU and AU sources and access dates; the page does not hold a separate dataset.

```bash
make site        # validates, builds and regenerates site/index.html
```

The static output lives in `site/`; run `make site` to generate `site/index.html` locally, or publish it with the workflow in `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. The generated HTML is not tracked, so a fresh clone rebuilds it from the records. Pages is already enabled with **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**; the workflow publishes on push to `main`. After the workflow runs, the page is served at `https://avvohacker.github.io/international-ai-law-corpus/`. The workflow rebuilds the catalogue and the atlas before uploading, so the published page cannot silently drift from the records.

The maps use Natural Earth 1:110m Admin 0 Countries (public domain) and the membership lists in `data/organisations.json`. No State names are drawn inside the maps. An international organisation that is a party is named below the map title, outside the map frame; its member States are shown in a lighter tone only where they did not act individually. The analytical labels “Washington / Beijing / Brussels–Strasbourg” are explicitly a reading of the record set, not a legal classification; the page says so.

## Verification levels

Every record declares one of:

- `full_text_checked` (10): a full text is stored, hashed, segmented and inspected; key passages are asserted in tests.
- `metadata_verified` (32): the official page or official excerpt was read during this session; a full text may be stored but was not read end-to-end.
- `source_located` (0): an official URL is recorded, but the metadata comes from an established reference and was not re-read in this session.

`metadata_verified` does **not** mean every field was independently verified. Party lists, reservations and some status details remain explicitly unknown. See [docs/methodology.md](docs/methodology.md).

## What is deliberately absent

- National legislation, except the EU AI Act, which is collected separately as supranational law.
- Academic literature and news commentary.
- A dynamic interface, API or server. The static GitHub Pages atlas is a generated view of the records, not a database-backed service.
- Automatic translations presented as authentic texts.
- Any claim to have searched the whole internet or to have found every existing instrument.
- General international law beyond the two background treaties (VCLT and ICCPR). The UN Charter, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the ECHR and Convention 108+ are not catalogued; this is a scope limit, not a finding that they are irrelevant to AI.
- Only one negotiation record (the CCW GGE on LAWS); there is no adopted draft protocol and no UN framework convention.

## Known limitations

1. **CETS 225 is not in force.** Its party table is captured at 23 November 2026 (21 signatories; Bosnia and Herzegovina and the EU have ratified), but no Conference of the Parties exists and implementation is not assessed. The Treaty Office publishes the chart dynamically and exposes no static export; a local JSON snapshot of the official chart/declarations is stored at `corpus/coe-cets-225/status/coe-cets-225-status-2026-11-23.json` and hashed in the manifest.
2. **Three official PDFs could not be acquired:** CETS 225 (HTTP 403; English text transcribed through an extraction tool), its Explanatory Report (403; metadata only) and the UNESCO Recommendation (403; metadata only). The official image/PDF is not stored for these.
3. **EUR-Lex PDFs returned HTTP 202/empty.** The EU AI Act and the EU OJ version of CETS 225 are catalogued with metadata, not stored full texts.
4. **Only English is stored** for texts. Other authentic language versions are recorded in metadata but not acquired.
5. **Most records are metadata-level.** 26 of 42 instruments are catalogued without a stored full text; 6 of the 16 stored texts were not read end-to-end. The distinctions are machine-readable.
6. **Only one negotiation record** (the CCW GGE); no adopted draft protocol, no Latin American or Middle Eastern regional AI instrument, and no bilateral AI treaty were verified.
7. **Printed pagination vs file pagination** is modelled only partially: unit `page_start`/`page_end` are PDF file pages; CETS 225 uses one logical page because its PDF was not acquired.

## Summary

This repository builds a first verifiable corpus of international AI law. It contains 42 catalogued instruments, 16 stored full texts and 15 originals with SHA-256 hashes. The two treaties specifically dedicated to AI are the Council of Europe Framework Convention (CETS 225), with 21 signatures and 2 ratified instruments, and the WAICO Establishment Agreement, with 38 reported founding members; neither is in force as of 23 November 2026. The CCW GGE on LAWS is the only record in category 6 (negotiations in progress), with no adopted instrument. The other binding agreements with AI provisions are digital trade agreements (DEPA, SADEA, UKSDEA, KSDPA, AfCFTA protocol), with cooperative rather than prescriptive clauses. The 2025 Paris Statement (63 States, the EU and the AU Commission; China and India endorsed, the United States and the UK did not), the 2023 Chinese initiative and the 2025 Pax Silica Declaration (26 signatories) document competing political coalitions without creating legal blocs. The atlas treats the three-pole image as a heuristic, not a legal classification: the seven maps, with the recorded parties listed under each map, show that treaty participation and political endorsement cut across it. The 2024 UK–US MOU and the US–EU TTC AI-safety dialogue commitment add the transatlantic layer: one bilateral MOU whose full text is unpublished, and one political commitment inside a broader joint statement. The corpus distinguishes binding and non-binding instruments, full texts and metadata-only records, verified and unknown data. It is not complete: gaps, searches performed and checks run are documented in `docs/`.
